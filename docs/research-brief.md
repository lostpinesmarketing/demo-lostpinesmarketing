# Research Brief: Lost Pines Marketing

Date: 2026-10-25  Site: https://lostpinesmarketing.com  Industry/location: Digital marketing and web design, Bastrop, TX

## 1. Snapshot
Lost Pines Marketing is a full-service digital marketing provider for businesses and non-profits. The site lists eight services: web development, email marketing, video and photography, event marketing, graphic design, e-commerce and payments, Google Business listings, and digital advertising. The only location given is Bastrop, TX. The logo carries "EST 2020" (source: logo file `lost-pines-marketing-b-retina.png`); no other founding detail appears anywhere public.

The site is a two-page WordPress install (Home, Contact) on the Divi theme. Home was last modified March 2022, Contact October 2022 (WordPress REST API, `/wp-json/wp/v2/pages`).

## 2. Owner's point of view
- **Goals (implied):** win local business and non-profit clients by offering one place for everything digital; book a free 30-minute consultation as the first step ("Setup a Free Meeting" is the only CTA).
- **How they describe themselves:** "Full Service Digital Marketing." Headline question: "Do you want your business or non-profit to have greater reach?" Copy is plain and practical ("We've got you covered from start to finish"), not jargon-heavy. Closing line: "Let's build something great, together."
- **What they likely protect:** the Lost Pines identity (pine-tree heptagon badge, the Lost Pines name tied to the Bastrop pine forest), a friendly partner tone, and breadth of service.
- **Likely frustrations with the current site (evidence):** the site looks like an unmodified Divi layout pack (purple gradient hero, coloured icon cards) and does not use the brand's own sage and charcoal palette; there is no working contact path; the site is hidden from search engines (see 6).

## 3. Audience point of view
- **Who they are:** small businesses and non-profits (named in the hero), likely in Bastrop and the surrounding Lost Pines area. Bastrop County grew 31.1% from 2010 to 2020 to about 97,000 people, with about 1,600 business establishments (Austin Chamber). Austin agencies now target Bastrop with dedicated landing pages, so local owners are being pitched by larger firms.
- **What they came to do:** see whether this provider handles their specific need (a new site, ads, email, an event), judge whether they are trustworthy and local, and book the free meeting.
- **What builds trust:** a real local presence, clear scope of services, a named person to talk to, examples of past work, reviews. Competitors lead with years in business, review counts and client logos.
- **What makes them leave:** no way to contact (currently true: no form, no email, no phone), a site that looks like a template (a red flag for a web design firm specifically), and a contact page that promises an email but shows none.

## 6. Current site assessment
- **Structure and navigation:** Home (hero, "Our Services" with 8 items, "Get Started" CTA) and Contact. Nav: Services (anchor), Contact. Two orphan WordPress pages exist ("Tickets Checkout", "Order Completed") from an events plugin, plus the default "Sample Page" and "Hello world!" post.
- **Strengths to keep (the 80%):** the eight services and their descriptions; the hero headline and question; "Let's build something great, together."; "free 30-minute consultation" offer; Bastrop, TX; simple two-page scope.
- **Gaps and problems (the 20%):**
  - Contact page has no form (a reCAPTCHA loads but no form renders) and no email, phone, or booking link. There is no way to reach the business.
  - `noindex, nofollow` on every page keeps the site out of Google.
  - Services are an undifferentiated list of 8 cards; no grouping or hierarchy.
  - No "who we are", no service area beyond the city name, no examples of work.
  - Design is the Divi layout pack defaults (purple-to-violet gradient, wave dividers, multicolour icon tiles), not the brand.
- **Mobile, speed, accessibility:** `user-scalable=0, maximum-scale=1.0` disables zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 issue); logo images have empty alt text; Divi plus Font Awesome plus ETmodules fonts load for a two-page site.
- **Brand assets found (all retrieved):**
  - Logo, white and charcoal versions (`lost-pines-marketing-w-retina.png`, `-b-retina.png`, 400x120)
  - Badge mark, charcoal and sage versions (`badge.png`, `badge-g.png`, 750x750): pine tree in a heptagon
  - Pine tree repeat pattern (`step-repeat-background.png`, sage)
  - Favicon (512x512)
  - Brand colours from those files: charcoal `#202322`, off-white `#F9F9F9`, sage `#7C9077`, light sage `#98AF91`, deep sage `#4E5A4D`
  - Fonts on the live site: Open Sans and Roboto (Divi defaults). The wordmark uses a bold condensed sans with a script "Marketing".
  - Not used: the rest of the media library is theme demo content (stock portraits, sample client logos, laptop mockup) and is not Lost Pines' own imagery.

## 7. Opportunities and recommendations
1. **Restore a working contact path.** A clear consultation CTA with a real email or booking link (evidence: contact page has neither).
2. **Rebuild the look on the brand's own palette and badge,** dropping the template gradient (evidence: logo/badge assets use sage and charcoal; the live site does not). For a web design firm the site is the portfolio.
3. **Group the eight services** into what clients are trying to do (build a presence vs. reach people) so a visitor finds their need faster.
4. **Own the local story:** Bastrop and the Lost Pines are the differentiator against Austin agencies with Bastrop landing pages.
5. **Remove noindex and pinch-zoom lock; add a Google Business listing and real trust signals** (reviews, work samples) once they exist. These are owner actions, not demo content.
